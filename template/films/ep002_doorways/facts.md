# EP002 "The Last Room": fact sheet

| Claim in script | Basis |
|---|---|
| In 2011, psychologist Gabriel Radvansky at the University of Notre Dame ran a test | Radvansky, G. A., Krawietz, S. A., & Tamplin, A. K. (2011). "Walking through doorways causes forgetting: Further explorations." *Quarterly Journal of Experimental Psychology*, 64(8), 1632–1645. Radvansky is at Notre Dame. (Earlier related work: Radvansky & Copeland, 2006, *Memory & Cognition*.) |
| People carried objects from one table to another, room after room | Exp. 1 (virtual environment): participants picked up an object at one table and carried it to another, either within a room or through a doorway. Exp. 2 used real rooms with objects carried in boxes. |
| Some walked through a doorway, others walked the same distance with no door | The design equated distance walked between the shift and no-shift conditions. |
| The ones who crossed a doorway forgot more | Memory for the carried object was worse after passing through a doorway (the "location updating effect"). |
| The brain cuts experience into scenes; psychologists call it an event boundary | Event Segmentation Theory (Zacks et al., 2007) and the event-horizon model (Radvansky, 2012): boundaries trigger updating of the current event model. |
| "Say what you came for out loud" / "leave the thought on the other side of a door" | Practical suggestions derived from the finding, presented as advice, not as tested results. |
