# EP001 "Open Loops": fact sheet

| Claim in script | Basis |
|---|---|
| In the 1920s, in a Berlin café/restaurant, a psychologist noticed a waiter remembering unpaid orders but forgetting paid ones | The standard account: Kurt Lewin observed this in a Berlin restaurant; his student Bluma Zeigarnik then studied it. The anecdote comes from secondary retellings; the script keeps it general ("a psychologist", "a waiter"). |
| "He never wrote anything down" | Part of the traditional anecdote (the waiter kept orders in memory). It is anecdotal, so it is phrased as narration, not as a statistic. |
| His student Bluma Zeigarnik took it into the lab, gave people simple tasks and interrupted half | Zeigarnik, B. (1927). "Über das Behalten von erledigten und unerledigten Handlungen." *Psychologische Forschung* 9, 1–85. Participants did ~20 short tasks; about half were interrupted. |
| They remembered the unfinished ones almost twice as well | Zeigarnik reported interrupted tasks recalled ~90% more often (ratio ≈ 1.9). Later replications are mixed, so the script says "almost twice" and describes her result only. |
| Making a specific plan quiets the loop | Masicampo, E. J., & Baumeister, R. F. (2011). "Consider it done! Plan making can eliminate the cognitive effects of unfulfilled goals." *JPSP* 101(4), 667–683. |
| "Start it for two minutes" | Generic productivity advice (not presented as research). |
